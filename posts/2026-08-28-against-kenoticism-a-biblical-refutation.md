In a recent article contributed by a friend on this blog, maintaining that Kenoticism has little to no historico-theological support in the past 1800 years of Christianity, writes, 

> While Kenoticism can certainly, to some degree, hold its ground through exegesis of the Scriptures, it was in the historical-theological aspect that it certainly cannot stand. Even proponents of this view admit, quite honestly, that this Christological teaching has little or no support in Christian antiquity.12 Thus, to rectify this doctrinal error, one must look back in the past and consider the faith handed down to us. The ecumenical creeds of the old, especially that of Chalcedon, do not allow, in any degree, a kenotic interpretation of the incarnation and of the Hypostatic Union. 

It is my aim in this article to put forth an exegetical refutation of Kenoticism while "allowing Chalcedon to be our lodestar in predicating Christ as the God-man" so that "all texts that seemingly favor a kenotic interpretation" are seen in the "light of the language of traditional Christian Christology" and clear and robust exegesis of the Scriptures.
