This essay from Abraham Booth is a great primer on the Kingdom of Christ. It provides a balanced outlook on the saints' pilgrimage on earth. A biblical distinction between the national, temporal, and earthly confederation of Israel, and the church's spiritual and eternal Kingdom informs the church's engagement with and expectation in the world, and performance of her duty. The key passage is "My Kingdom is not of the world" (John 18:36).

Here are the book's main divisions:

1. The Gospel Church is a kingdom not of this world, with regard to its **ORIGIN**.
2. The kingdom of Christ is not of this world, respecting the **SUBJECTS** of his righteous **GOVERNMENT**.
3. The kingdom of Christ is not of this world, in regard to the **LAWS** by which it is governed.
4. The kingdom of Christ is not like the empires of this world in regard to **EXTERNAL SPLENDOR**.
5. The kingdom of Christ is not of this world, relative to its **IMMUNITIES**, its **RICHES**, and its **HONOURS**.
6. The kingdom of Christ is not like the dominions of secular princes, with regard to its **LIMITS** and its **DURATION**.

Here are some quotes:

> "The empire of Christ, indeed, extends to every creature for 'all authority in heaven and on earth' is in his hands, and he 'is Head over all things to the Church.' But the kingdom of which we treat, stands distinguished from that of general Providence, as well as from every political state."

> "It is, indeed, the indispensable duty of secular princes, and of their people, to love and adore God yet that obligation arises, not from any political relation subsisting between them, but from their being reasonable creatures. (12)"

> "That National Constitution being abolished, Jehovah's political sovereignty is at an end. The Covenant therefore now in force, and the royal relation of our Lord to the church, are entirely spiritual. (23)"

> "As none but regenerate persons belong to the kingdom of Christ, no one is better subject of his dominion, or more honourable member of his church, on account of wealth or power; neither pertains to the true glory of a Christian church, nor to the sterling worth of a Christian character. For what concern have worldly wealth and civil power, in forming a spiritual character, or in adorning a spiritual kingdom? (27)"

> "If our Lord considers himself as honoured by the preacher's labours, and if the minister has any reason to expect success, it must be by a faithful and simple promulgation of revealed truths. (30)"

> "It is frequently much easier for people, and much more desired by them, to assemble in an elegant edifice, and for their ministers to appear in canonical fashion; than to perform a spiritual worship, and to shine in the beauties of holiness. (51)"

> "The New Testament Church being so frequently denominated, the Kingdom of Heaven, and The Kingdom of God, very plainly indicates, that the Messiah is a Divine Person, and truly God; for by these high appellations, we are naturally led to consider it as a kingdom in which GOD HIMSELF REIGNS. (61)"

> "Our Lord has promised, indeed, that their obedience to his royal pleasure shall meet with his gracious regards in the present life. Not by indulging them with temporal riches, or by granting them, external honour and ease; but by admitting them into more intimate communion with himself, and by rejoicing their hearts with his favour. (59)"

> "…the glory of the divine perfections appears and shines in the unveiled face of Jesus Christ… that this glory is beheld by New Testament believers; and that, by beholding it, they are gradually transformed into the glorious image of God. (66)"

> "Thus we are habituated to a kind of celestial service, by which our likeness to Christ is promoted, and our desires after heaven increased. In these things the very life of spiritual worship and of real religion consists. He therefore is not worthy to be called a subject of our Lord's kingdom, who is not habitually aiming, in his devotional services, at this delightful and solemn intercourse with God. Nor is he deserving of that exalted character, whose thoughts and cares, whose hopes and fears, whose joys and sorrows are not principally concerned about the government and grace of Christ, considered in their connection with the heavenly state. (68)"

> "'So various and so great are the defects in our devotional services, that we might well despair, were it not for a High Priest who bears the iniquity of our "holy things." For we "find a law, that when we would do good, evil is present with us." (70)'"
