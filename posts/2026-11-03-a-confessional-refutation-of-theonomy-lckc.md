The L-C-K-C framework — Law's Division, Covenantal Context, Kingdom Distinction, and Church's Mission — provides a complete confessional refutation of theonomy, grounded primarily in Justin Perdue's confessional Baptist analysis.[^1]

---

## I. L — Law's Division (Threefold & Positive vs. Moral Law)

**Theonomist Position**

> "We must recognize the continuing obligation of civil magistrates to obey and enforce the relevant laws of the Old Testament, including the penal sanctions specified by the just Judge of all the earth. As with the rest of God's law, we must presume continuity of binding authority regarding the socio-political commandments revealed as standing law in the Old Testament."[^2]

> "The moral law of God can... be seen in two subdivisions, the divisions having simply a literary difference: (1) general or summary precepts of morality... and (2) commands that specify the general precepts by way of illustrative application."[^3]

**Scripture Proofs**

> "For when Gentiles, who do not have the law, by nature do what the law requires, they are a law to themselves, even though they do not have the law. They show that the work of the law is written on their hearts, while their conscience also bears witness…" — Romans 2:14–15

> "Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them…" — Matthew 5:17–18

**Confessional & Refutational Quotes**

> "Confessional Baptist doctrine maintains a threefold division of the law, as well as a distinction between moral and positive law... Israel's ceremonial and judicial laws were all positive laws. Israel would not have known these ceremonial or judicial laws if God had not revealed them."[^1]

> "To them also he gave sundry judicial Laws, which expired together with the state of that people, not obliging any now by virtue of that institution; their general equity only, being of moral use."[^4]

> "For the Lord through the hand of Moses did not give that law to be proclaimed among all nations and to be in force everywhere…"[^5]

---

## II. C — Covenantal Context (1689 Federalism & Noahic Basis)

**Theonomist Position**

> "God's law as revealed in Scripture ought to direct society and the civil magistrate today. A society which is to reflect Christian morality is a society which has the full, distinctive law of God for its direction."[^6]

**Scripture Proofs**

> "Whoever sheds the blood of man, by man shall his blood be shed, for God made man in his own image." — Genesis 9:6

> "In speaking of a new covenant, he makes the first one obsolete. And what is becoming obsolete and growing old is ready to vanish away." — Hebrews 8:13

**Confessional & Refutational Quotes**

> "It is the Noahic covenant, not the Mosaic, that provides the basis for the function of civil government. The proportionate, retributive justice articulated by the Noahic covenant was 'an eye for an eye.' If you harm someone physically or financially, the civil authorities should exact an equal and just penalty for the sake of the victim."[^1]

> "The Mosaic judicial law was specific and circumstantial. It was specific insofar as it had a narrow scope of sustaining Israel in Canaan as it moved toward the birth of Christ. It was circumstantial insofar as it only applied to that era of redemptive history... Once Christ fulfilled the old and established the new, the uniquely situated old covenant judicial laws were no longer needed."[^1]

> "Positive laws are tethered to their respective covenants, and the positive laws of the Mosaic covenant are not binding under the new covenant."[^1]

---

## III. K — Kingdom Distinction (Two Kingdoms & Pilgrim Identity)

**Theonomist Position**

> "…the Christian is obligated to keep the whole law of God as a pattern of sanctification and that this law is to be enforced by the civil magistrate where and how the stipulations of God so designate. That is to say, 'theonomy' has a central and irradicable place in any genuinely Christian ethic…"[^7]

**Scripture Proofs**

> "Jesus answered, 'My kingdom is not of this world. If my kingdom were of this world, my servants would have been fighting, that I might not be delivered over to the Jews. But my kingdom is not from the world.'" — John 18:36

> "Therefore render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's." — Matthew 22:21

**Confessional & Refutational Quotes**

> "A confessional perspective on the sufficiency of Scripture, two-kingdoms doctrine, the mission of the church, and the Christian as pilgrim runs counter to the assertions of theonomy."[^1]

> "The Christian life is a pilgrimage. We're sojourners and exiles in this world, and we face thousands of spiritual dangers… Yet our calling is not to turn Babylon into Jerusalem."[^1]

> "Should Christians pick up the sword to accomplish civil ends? Yes. But ecclesial ones? No…"[^1]

---

## IV. C — Church's Mission (Gospel & Sacraments vs. Cultural Dominion)

**Theonomist Position**

> "Theonomy tends to redefine the mission of the church to include adopting civil governments, enforcing the old covenant judicial code, building a Christian society, and Christianizing the nations."[^1]

> "The Commission [of Matt. 28:18–20] states that believers are to extend Christ's kingdom, making disciples in all nations by going, baptizing, and teaching. This mandate is the essence of the New Covenant which is but an extension of the Old Covenant: Go and claim everything in heaven and on earth for the Kingdom, taking dominion for His Name's sake (Genesis 1:28)."[^8]

**Scripture Proofs**

> "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you…" — Matthew 28:19–20

> "First of all, then, I urge that supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings be made for all people, for kings and all who are in high positions, that we may lead a peaceful and quiet life, godly and dignified in every way." — 1 Timothy 2:1–2

**Confessional & Refutational Quotes**

> "The new covenant, however, gives the church a narrower mission. We have a mandate, but it's not dominionistic. Rather, Christ commands the church to preach the gospel and rightly administer the sacraments for the salvation of God's people. He promises to build his church through Word and sacrament, through the preaching of law and gospel, through the ordinary means of grace."[^1]

> "As a result, theonomy obscures the gospel — the forgiveness of sins, the imputation of Christ's righteousness, and the guarantee of eternal life on account of him alone. Wherever theonomy wins the day, these truths get fuzzy, or at least downplayed."[^1]

> "The church exists in order to change the subject from us and our deeds to God and his deeds of salvation, from our various missions to save the world to Christ's mission that has already accomplished redemption."[^9]

---

In summary, the L-C-K-C framework demonstrates how historic confessional theology refutes theonomy by upholding positive law expiration, locating civil justice in the Noahic covenant, preserving the two-kingdoms distinction for pilgrim believers, and restricting the church's mission strictly to Word and sacrament.[^1]
